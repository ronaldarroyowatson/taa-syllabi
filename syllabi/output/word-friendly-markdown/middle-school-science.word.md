# Middle School Science Syllabus
## Term
Fall Semester 2026

## Course Header
- Grades: 7-8

- Textbook: ByDesign Science by Carol Raney, K, and colleagues.

## Course Description
Middle School Science provides a broad introduction to life science, physical science, and Earth science. Students develop foundational scientific skills through hands-on experiments, modeling, observation, and inquiry-based learning. The course emphasizes curiosity, problem-solving, and real-world applications while preparing students for high-school level science.

## Units
- 8th Grade Book Material (current-year rotation).
- 8th Grade Unit 1 Life Science: Chapter 1 How Life on Earth Has Changed; Chapter 2 Types of Plants; Chapter 3 How Plants Survive; Chapter 4 How Animals Survive.
- 8th Grade Unit 2 The Human Body: Chapter 5 Genes and Disease; Chapter 6 Your Lifestyle Affects Your Body; Chapter 7 Making Decisions about Your Body.
- 8th Grade Unit 3 Earth and Space Science: Chapter 8 The Atmosphere, Weather, and Climate; Chapter 9 Resources and Their Uses; Chapter 10 Earth's History.
- 8th Grade Unit 4 Physical Science: Chapter 11 Ways Matter Changes; Chapter 12 Elements and the Periodic Table; Chapter 13 Types of Chemical Bonds; Chapter 14 Chemical Reactions.
- 7th Grade Book Material (next-year rotation).
- 7th Grade Unit 1 Life Science: Chapter 1 Bacteria, Protists, and Fungi; Chapter 2 Characteristics of Invertebrates; Chapter 3 Characteristics of Vertebrates; Chapter 4 How Organisms Inherit Traits.
- 7th Grade Unit 2 The Human Body: Chapter 5 Human Development and Sexuality; Chapter 6 Infectious Diseases; Chapter 7 Other Systems of the Human Body.
- 7th Grade Unit 3 Earth and Space Science: Chapter 8 Objects in Our Solar System; Chapter 9 The Effects of Earth's Movement; Chapter 10 Stars, Galaxies, and the Universe.
- 7th Grade Unit 4 Physical Science: Chapter 11 Energy Waves; Chapter 12 Motion and Forces; Chapter 13 Work, Power, and Machines; Chapter 14 Energy Transferred and Transformed.

## Shared Grading
## Grades and Assignment Rules
### Homework and Classwork
- Turn in work by the class deadline.
- Late work can be marked missing until you submit it.
- Missing work may still be accepted during the grading period.
- Finish all parts of your assignment so it can be fully graded.
- Late or missing work is often graded after on-time work.

### Incomplete Assignments
- Incomplete work counts as zero until finished.
- If the missing part was an honest mistake, full credit may be possible.
- If work was incomplete because of poor effort or time use, partial credit may be given.

### Grading Philosophy
- Grades should show what you have learned.
- You are responsible for keeping up with work.
- Sometimes your teacher may use a different but equal assignment to check learning.
- In special cases, grades may be changed to better match your understanding.

## Shared Policies
## Academic and Class Policies
### Academic Honesty
- Do your own work.
- Do not copy, cheat, or use someone else's work as your own.
- Do not use hidden notes or share answers during quizzes and tests.
- Cheating can lead to a zero, a parent call, and office consequences.
- Your teacher may allow a supervised redo in some cases.

### Substitute Assignments
- Some students may get alternate assignments for learning needs.
- Alternate work must cover the same skills and depth.
- Alternate quizzes, tests, or projects must still meet deadlines unless approved.
- If a required group project is missed for a school reason, an equal replacement may be used.
